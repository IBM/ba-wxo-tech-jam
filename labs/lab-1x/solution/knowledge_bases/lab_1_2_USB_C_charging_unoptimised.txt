USB-C Charging — Overview and Troubleshooting

This article covers common charging questions for Focus Corp laptop products. It is intended
for internal support staff and should be read in conjunction with the product-specific warranty
and hardware repair guidelines. For escalation procedures, see the Hardware Escalation Policy
document. For cable and adapter procurement, refer to the Accessories Catalogue.

Background

USB-C has become the standard charging interface for modern laptops due to its versatility.
Unlike proprietary barrel connectors, USB-C supports both data transfer and power delivery over
the same cable. However, not all USB-C cables and adapters are equal — power delivery capability
depends on the cable standard, the adapter wattage rating, and the laptop's firmware negotiation
protocol. Incompatible combinations can result in the laptop refusing to charge, charging slowly,
or appearing to charge without the battery level actually increasing.

For Focus Corp's current laptop lineup, charging behaviour and port specifications are documented
in the individual product data sheets. Support agents should verify the model number before
advising on compatible accessories.

Common Issues Reported

Customers frequently contact support about the following charging-related symptoms: the laptop
does not appear to charge when plugged in, the charging LED does not illuminate, the battery
percentage stays the same or decreases while connected, intermittent charging behaviour where
the connection drops in and out, and the laptop powers off unexpectedly even when the charger
is plugged in.

In most cases these symptoms point to one of three root causes: an incompatible or underpowered
adapter, a damaged cable or port, or a power source issue. A small proportion of cases involve
firmware or battery hardware faults that require escalation.

Troubleshooting

When a customer reports a charging issue, agents should work through the following checks in
order. Do not skip steps or reorder them — the sequence is designed to rule out the most common
causes first before escalating.

First, check the adapter. Confirm that the customer is using a USB-C adapter that supports
laptop-level power delivery. Many phone and tablet chargers use the same USB-C connector but
deliver far less wattage than a laptop requires. The Focus Gamer Z2, for example, needs at
least 150 W — a 65 W phone charger plugged into this model will typically result in the laptop
drawing from the battery even while connected. Ask the customer to check the wattage printed
on their adapter or to locate the model name and look it up. If they are unsure, recommend
testing with the adapter that came in the box.

Second, inspect the cable and port. USB-C cables that support power delivery are marked with
the USB PD symbol or the Thunderbolt logo. Standard data-only cables will not carry enough
current. Ask the customer to try a different cable if they have one available. Also ask them
to inspect the USB-C port on the laptop for visible debris, bent pins, or signs of physical
damage. A compressed-air canister can be used to clear dust.

Third, eliminate the power source as a variable. Have the customer try a different wall outlet
and plug the charger directly into the wall rather than through a power strip, extension lead,
or docking station. Surge-protected strips and older extension leads can introduce resistance
that reduces available power.

Fourth, check the charging state after a few minutes. Some laptops do not show a charging
indicator immediately if the battery is fully depleted. Ask the customer to leave the laptop
connected for 15 minutes with the lid closed before checking again. A restart while connected
can also trigger the firmware to re-negotiate the power delivery session.

Fifth, reduce the power load. High-performance tasks running in the background can consume
power faster than a marginal charger can supply it, causing the battery to drain even while
plugged in. Ask the customer to close unnecessary applications and disconnect any USB peripherals,
then observe whether the charging indicator appears.

If none of these steps resolve the issue, the case should be escalated to the hardware repair
team. Document the steps attempted and the customer's responses before escalating.
